10.3Measuring purity (entropy)

Turning “how mixed up is this group?” into a single number between 0 and 1. The one genuinely new piece of maths this week.

C2 · Week 4 · lesson 3 of 13124 of 177 · 70% of the book

❓Before you read

A bag of 6 cats and 0 dogs versus 3 cats and 3 dogs. Which is messier — and can you put a number on the mess?

I have committed to an answer

Watch for the number being 0 and 1 at the two extremes, and for why all-dogs is just as tidy as all-cats.

🎈The idea, in plain words

In plain English

A bag of 6 cats and 0 dogs: reach in, and you already know what you’ll get. Zero surprise. Zero mess. Call it 0.

A bag of 3 cats and 3 dogs: you have no idea what’s coming out. Maximum surprise. Maximum mess. Call it 1.

Entropy is the number that measures exactly this: how surprised will I be by the next thing I pull out? And notice it is symmetric — all cats and all dogs are both perfectly tidy.

🔢The maths, decoded

entropy — impurity of a group — hover or click a part
H(p) = −p log2(p)log, base 2 → bits − (1 − p) log2(1 − p)the other class, same idea
SymbolSay it out loudWhat it actually is
p“p one”The fraction of the group that is the positive class. 4 cats out of 5 → p = 0.8.
log2“log base two”Base 2 makes H come out in bits, so a 50/50 group is exactly 1 — one coin flip of uncertainty.
the minus signs“flip it positive”log of a number below 1 is negative. The minus makes H positive, so bigger = messier.
H = 0“pure”Everything is the same class. Happens at p = 0 and at p = 1.
H = 1“total mess”Exactly half and half. p = 0.5.
0 log 0“defined as 0”Mathematically it is a limit; in code you must special-case it or you get NaN.
🔧If you have never seen a formula

Sorting a drawer of mixed screws.

A drawer that is all one size needs no thought — reach in blind and you have the right screw. A drawer half wood-screws and half machine-screws makes you look every time. Entropy is a number for exactly that: how much looking a group forces on you.

🌉If you know a neighbouring field

This is Shannon entropy, from information theory, and it measures surprise — the average number of bits needed to convey which class an item is.

All-one-class costs zero bits: you never need to be told. A fifty-fifty split costs exactly one bit per item, which is why the maximum is 1 and why the logarithm is base 2. The units are literally bits.

👁One thing to picture

An arch, upside down.

Zero at both ends — all one class, or all the other — and peaking dead centre at a 50/50 mix. Note that H(0.8) and H(0.2) are identical: entropy measures how mixed, not which side wins.

⚖️Where this really costs something

The same formula compresses your files. ZIP and JPEG work by spending fewer bits on predictable data and more on surprising data — which is Shannon’s result applied to storage rather than to splitting.

A decision tree asking “which question most reduces entropy?” is asking “which question compresses my uncertainty most?”. The two fields are the same mathematics with different goals.

So the formula below is measuring surprise in bits, and the tree simply hunts for whichever question removes the most of it.

The symmetry is worth pausing on: H(0.8) = H(0.2). Entropy does not care which class dominates, only how lopsided the mix is. That is exactly what you want from a purity measure.

Gini vs entropy

Many libraries default to the Gini impurity, 2p(1−p), instead. It is cheaper to compute (no logarithm), has almost the same shape, and gives nearly identical trees. Entropy is taught because it has a clean information-theoretic meaning; Gini is used because it is fast.

🕳Traps

Common trap

log(0) in code. 0 * log(0) is mathematically 0 but numerically NaN. Guard it: if p == 0 or p == 1: return 0.

Common trap

Using the wrong log base. Base 2 gives bits and a maximum of 1. Natural log gives nats and a maximum of 0.693. Splits come out identical either way — only the numbers on the page change.

Say it out loud

Base 2 gives a maximum entropy of 1 and natural log gives 0.693, and the splits chosen come out identical. Say why the base cannot change which split wins.

said it — now check

Changing the base multiplies every entropy by the same constant. Information gain is a difference of entropies, so it is scaled by that same constant too — and scaling every candidate's score by one positive number cannot change which is largest.

So the base is a choice of units, exactly like measuring in metres or feet. Bits are conventional because they carry the clean information-theoretic reading: a 50/50 group costs one coin flip of uncertainty.

✅Check yourself

A node has 6 cats and 2 not-cats. What is p, and roughly what is H?

p = 6/8 = 0.75. H(0.75) = −0.75log₂0.75 − 0.25log₂0.25 ≈ 0.811.

Why is H(0.8) equal to H(0.2)?

Because the formula is symmetric about p = 0.5. Impurity depends on how lopsided the mix is, not which class is on top.

Group A: 100 examples, all cats. Group B: 2 examples, both cats. Same entropy?

Yes — both are 0. Entropy measures purity, not size. That is why information gain weights each branch by how many examples it holds.

  1. From quiz question 1 · entropy

    RecallClose the lesson. Pulling it out of memory is what makes it stay; re-reading only makes it feel familiar.

    From a blank page: write the two-class entropy H(p), say what p is, and give H at p = 0, 0.5 and 1. Why is H(0.8) the same as H(0.2)?

    I have written it down — check me

    H(p) = −p log2(p) − (1 − p) log2(1 − p), where p is the fraction of the node in one class.

    • H(0) = 0 and H(1) = 0: a pure node, no uncertainty.
    • H(0.5) = 1: the most mixed a two-class node can be.

    The formula is symmetric in p and 1 − p. Entropy measures how mixed a node is, not which class is winning.

    Say it three ways
    In one line

    Entropy is 0 when a group is all one kind and 1 when it is a perfect half-and-half mix.

    Like you are ten

    If every sweet in the bag is a lemon one, you know exactly what you will pull out: no surprise. If half are lemon and half are cherry, every pull is a surprise. Entropy is how surprised you expect to be.

    entropya number for how mixed up a group is
    pure nodea group where every example is the same class
    log2the logarithm in base 2, which makes the answer come out in bits

    Work itPen and paper, real numbers. You do not own a formula until you have pushed numbers through it yourself.

    Compute H for nodes with these (cats, dogs) counts: (10, 0), (7, 3), (3, 7), (9, 1), (5, 5).

    Which two come out equal, and why?

    show the working
    • (10, 0): p = 1, H = 0
    • (7, 3): p = 0.7, H = 0.881
    • (3, 7): p = 0.3, H = 0.881
    • (9, 1): p = 0.9, H = 0.469
    • (5, 5): p = 0.5, H = 1

    (7, 3) and (3, 7) are equal: the mix is the same, only the majority has swapped. (10, 0) is pure; (5, 5) is the maximum. Note how fast H rises: a node that is 90% one class still has entropy 0.469, not 0.1.

    Say it three ways
    In one line

    Entropy depends only on the mix, and it climbs steeply as soon as a node stops being pure.

    Like you are ten

    A bag with one dog among nine cats is still quite a surprising bag, more than you might guess.

  3. From quiz question 1 · entropy

    Spot the errorThis working looks right and is not. Find the exact line that fails before you open the answer — this is the skill a quiz distractor tests.

    Someone codes entropy straight from the formula and tests it on a pure node:

    p = 1.0
H = -p * np.log2(p) - (1 - p) * np.log2(1 - p)

    It prints nan with a warning. Where is the mistake, and what should a pure node give?

    show me where it breaks

    The second term is 0 × log2(0) = 0 × (−∞), which the computer calls nan. But the formula is meant to use the convention 0 log 0 = 0 — the limit of p log p as p shrinks to 0.

    A pure node should give 0. The fix is to return 0 whenever p is 0 or 1 before taking any logs.

    Say it three ways
    In one line

    A class with no members contributes nothing to entropy, even though log 0 is not a number.

    Like you are ten

    If there are no cherry sweets at all, they cannot surprise you. The formula needs to be told that, because on its own it trips over the empty group.

    The words, in simple words
    nan“not a number” — what a computer gives for 0 times infinity
    conventionan agreed rule for an edge case
    limitthe value something heads towards as you get closer and closer

    TransferA situation the lesson never showed. If the idea only works on the lesson's own example, you have memorised the example, not the idea.

    A fruit-sorting node holds four kinds of fruit. Entropy generalises to H = −Σ pk log2 pk. Compute it for an equal four-way mix, for a node with only two kinds in equal amounts, and for 70%% / 10%% / 10%% / 10%%. Is 1 still the ceiling?

    compare with my answer
    • Four equal kinds: H = 2 bits.
    • Two equal kinds: H = 1 bit.
    • 70 / 10 / 10 / 10: H ≈ 1.357 bits.

    No: with k classes the ceiling is log2 k, so 2 bits for four. The “between 0 and 1” rule is a two-class fact, not a property of entropy.

    Say it three ways
    In one line

    Entropy's maximum grows with the number of classes: log base 2 of how many there are.

    Like you are ten

    Guessing a fruit from four kinds is harder than guessing a coin from two sides, so the surprise score can go higher.
