9.12Adding data

When you do need more data, you rarely need more of everything. Targeted collection, augmentation, and synthesis — cheapest first.

C2 · Week 3 · lesson 12 of 17116 of 177 · 66% of the book

❓Before you read

You need more data and labelling is expensive. Guess two ways to get more without collecting any.

I have committed to an answer

Watch for augmentation and synthesis, and for the rule about what a useful distortion looks like.

🎈The idea, in plain words

In plain English

You need more practice questions. Three ways to get them:

  1. Buy more — but only of the type you keep getting wrong. (targeted collection)
  2. Bend the ones you have — turn the page sideways, photocopy it badly, read it in dim light. Still the same question, still the same answer. (augmentation)
  3. Make brand new ones from scratch — write your own with a computer. (synthesis)
🔧If you have never seen a formula

Teaching someone to recognise your handwriting. You could write a thousand more words. Or you could write the same words with a blunt pencil, on lined paper, in poor light, at speed.

The second gives them something the first cannot: what your handwriting looks like when conditions are bad, which is exactly when they will need to read it.

🌉If you know a neighbouring field

Data augmentation is the systematic version, and the rule for a valid augmentation is precise: the distortion must be one that plausibly occurs in deployment.

Rotating a photograph by 15° is realistic; adding uniform random pixel noise generally is not, and the second reliably fails to help. That test — would this really happen? — is the whole design criterion.

👁One thing to picture

One training image, and beside it eight copies: rotated, brighter, blurred, cropped, mirrored.

Nine examples from one label. The label cost you nothing to reuse, which is the entire economic argument, since labelling is almost always the expensive part.

⚖️Where this really costs something

Augmentation is why modern vision and speech systems work at the data volumes actually available. Speech models are trained on clean audio deliberately mixed with car noise, crowd noise and bad phone lines.

Mirroring, though, is a good example of how domain-specific the choice is: fine for cats, catastrophic for handwritten digits, where a mirrored 2 is not a 2.

So the augmentations below are not tricks. They are statements about what your deployment environment does to your inputs.

🔢The three strategies

StrategyWhat it isCostBest for
targeted collectionget more of the category error analysis flaggedmedium — but focusedwhen one category dominates your errors
data augmentationdistort existing examples in realistic wayscheapimages, audio — anything with natural variation
data synthesisgenerate brand-new examples artificiallymedium–highOCR (render text in many fonts), simulation, rare events
The one thing to remember

The distortion must be representative of what happens in the real world. Adding random per-pixel noise to clean scanned text teaches the model to solve a problem it will never encounter — pure wasted capacity.

🔬Examples that work

Images

Rotate, crop, flip, scale, change brightness and contrast, add realistic blur. Flipping is fine for cats; not for handwritten digits, where a mirrored 2 is not a 2.

Audio

Add crowd noise, car noise, a poor phone line, slight speed changes. One clip becomes a dozen realistic recordings.

Text (OCR)

Render real words in thousands of fonts, sizes, colours and backgrounds. Photorealistic synthesis is a huge win here.

📊The data-centric shift

The classical approach fixes the data and iterates on the model. The data-centric approach fixes the model and iterates on the data — usually by improving label quality and consistency rather than adding volume.

On many practical problems, with a decent architecture already available, improving the data is the higher-return activity. Andrew has spent much of the last few years arguing exactly this.

🔤The words, decoded

SymbolSay it out loudWhat it actually is
data augmentation“augmentation”Making new training examples by distorting existing ones. The distortion must resemble noise that actually occurs.
data synthesis“synthesis”Manufacturing examples from scratch — rendering text in many fonts, for instance.
data-centric“data-centric”Improving the data and holding the model fixed, rather than the reverse. Often the better return.
transfer learning“transfer learning”Starting from a model trained on someone else's much larger data set.

🕳Traps

Common trap

Augmenting with distortions that never happen. If your production images are always upright, rotating them 90° adds noise, not signal.

Common trap

Augmenting the cross-validation or test set. Only augment the training set. Your evaluation must reflect the real distribution.

Common trap

Adding data to fix high bias. Still doesn’t work. Check the diagnostic first.

Say it out loud

Augmenting the training set helps; augmenting the cross-validation set is a mistake. Say why the same operation is right in one place and wrong in the other.

said it — now check

The training set is what the model learns from, so adding realistic variation teaches it about conditions it will meet. The evaluation sets exist to estimate performance on the real distribution, and augmenting them changes the distribution you are measuring against.

You would end up reporting accuracy on rotated, blurred, brightened images that nobody will actually send you — a number about a world that does not exist. Distort what the model learns from; never distort the yardstick.

✅Check yourself

Speech recogniser struggles with car noise. Cheapest fix?

Augmentation: overlay recorded car noise onto your existing clean clips. Far cheaper than recording thousands of new in-car samples.

You mirror-flip images of handwritten digits. What goes wrong?

A mirrored 2 is not a 2. The label is no longer correct, so you are training on wrong answers. Flipping is only valid where the class is genuinely symmetric.

When is targeted collection better than augmentation?

When error analysis shows one specific category dominating and you cannot realistically simulate it — for example, a rare defect type you have only five photos of.
