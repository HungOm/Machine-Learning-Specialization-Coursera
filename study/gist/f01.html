The gist — The Maths You Actually Need

Nineteen lessons, and every one of them is a part of the same machine. This page shows the machine.

What this page is for

The 19 lesson pages for this week each teach one idea properly. That is the right shape for meeting an idea and the wrong shape for seeing how the ideas join — so this page is only the joins. Nothing here is new: if a claim on this page is not already in one of those lessons, it is a bug.

Read it after the week for consolidation, or before it as a map you will recognise on the way through. Both work; reading it instead of the week does not.

Nineteen separate-looking topics, and they are not nineteen topics. They are the parts of one sentence: add up how wrong you are, work out which way is downhill, and step that way. Every symbol in this week appears somewhere in that sentence.

🗣The three symbols people stall on

The one thing to remember

If any of the notation is still opaque, it is almost always one of these three, and each has a one-line fix.

Σ is a for loop somebody wrote down. Cover it with your thumb, read what is to the right, then remember it happens once per example and gets added up.

∂ is a d that is announcing “there are other variables and I am holding them still”. It is not a different kind of derivative.

x(i) with round brackets is example i, never a power. The bracket style carries the meaning, every time, throughout the specialization.

🚧What this week deliberately leaves out

Common trap

No proofs. You are learning to read and use this notation, not to derive it. That is the right trade for what comes next.

No integration. Calculus here is one-directional: derivatives only. You will not need the other half.

No linear algebra beyond shapes. Eigenvectors and SVD wait for Week 3, and only because PCA needs them.

Nothing about learning yet. This week has no model, no cost and no data. It is the alphabet, not the language.
