C4 · Week 2 · 7 problems · 1 warm-up · 5 core · 1 stretch

Problems — Attention

Almost all of these use one two-token example, small enough to check on paper. Work through the first three in order — they are one calculation split into three, and doing it by hand once is worth more than reading it five times.

How to use this page

Work each one on paper, with the solution shut. Open the hint only after you are genuinely stuck, and the solution only after you have written something down — even a wrong something. Reading a worked solution you have not attempted feels like learning and is not.

Then grade yourself honestly. A missed problem pulls that lesson’s review cards forward to tomorrow and shows up in weak spots.

1warm-upscores↗ lesson
Use this two-token example throughout: Q = [[1,1],[2,0]], K = [[1,1],[0,2]], V = [[4,0],[0,8]], and d_k = 2.
Compute QK^T — the raw score matrix.
worked solution
In plain wordsDot each row of Q with each row of K. Four dot products, four numbers.
  1. q₁ · k₁[1,1]·[1,1] = 1 + 1 = 2
  2. q₁ · k₂[1,1]·[0,2] = 0 + 2 = 2
  3. q₂ · k₁[2,0]·[1,1] = 2 + 0 = 2
  4. q₂ · k₂
    your turn[2,0]·[0,2] = 0 + 0 = 0
answerQK^T = [[2, 2], [2, 0]]
What this is really testingRows are queries (who is asking), columns are keys (who is offering). Keeping that straight is most of what it takes to read attention code.
2corescale and softmax↗ lesson
Use this two-token example throughout: Q = [[1,1],[2,0]], K = [[1,1],[0,2]], V = [[4,0],[0,8]], and d_k = 2.
Scale those scores by √d_k and softmax each row. Verify each row sums to 1.
stuck? one hint
√2 = 1.4142. Softmax each row on its own — never down a column.
worked solution
  1. Scale row 1[2, 2] ÷ 1.4142 = [1.4142, 1.4142]
  2. Softmax row 1both equal, so the weights split evenly → [0.5, 0.5]
  3. Scale row 2[2, 0] ÷ 1.4142 = [1.4142, 0]
  4. Exponentiate
    your turne^1.4142 = 4.1133, e^0 = 1, sum = 5.1133
  5. Divide
    your turn4.1133/5.1133 = 0.8044, 1/5.1133 = 0.1956
answerRow 1: [0.5000, 0.5000]. Row 2: [0.8044, 0.1956]. Both sum to exactly 1.
A second way to see itRow 1's two scores were equal, so an even split is the only sensible answer — a good sanity check that you softmaxed the right axis.
What this is really testingRows summing to 1 is the check that catches the single most common attention bug. Softmaxing columns runs without error and produces meaningless output.
3corethe output↗ lesson
Use this two-token example throughout: Q = [[1,1],[2,0]], K = [[1,1],[0,2]], V = [[4,0],[0,8]], and d_k = 2.
Using the weights from the previous problem, compute the final output.
worked solution
  1. Output row 10.5×[4,0] + 0.5×[0,8]
  2. = [2, 0] + [0, 4] = [2, 4]
  3. Output row 20.8044×[4,0] + 0.1956×[0,8]
  4. your turn= [3.2177, 0] + [0, 1.5646] = [3.2177, 1.5646]
answeroutput = [[2.000, 4.000], [3.218, 1.565]]
A second way to see itPosition 1 split its attention evenly and got an even blend of the two values. Position 2 leaned on the first value and its output reflects that.
What this is really testingNote the output has two rows — the same as the input. That shape preservation is exactly what lets attention layers stack twelve deep.
4coreshapes↗ lesson
A sequence has 64 positions, model width d = 512, and d_k = d_v = 64. What are the shapes of Q, of QK^T, and of the attention output? Which dimension disappears, and why?
worked solution
  1. Q = XW_Q, where X is (64, 512) and W_Q is (512, 64)Q is (64, 64)
  2. QK^T: (64, 64) @ (64, 64)^Tthe inner 64 (d_k) meets and vanishes → (64, 64)
  3. Output: weights @ V, (64, 64) @ (64, 64)
    your turn(64, 64)
answerQ is (64, 64), QK^T is (64, 64), output is (64, 64). The dimension that disappears is d_k — it was the inner dimension of the multiply, so the score grid depends only on how many positions there are.
What this is really testingIt is a coincidence here that T and d_k are both 64. Redo it with T = 100 and d_k = 64 and you get a (100, 100) score grid — the shape rule is what tells you that.
5corescaling↗ lesson
Query and key components are roughly independent with variance 1. Explain why the standard deviation of their dot product is √d_k, and what goes wrong at d_k = 512 if you skip the division.
stuck? one hint
A dot product is a sum of d_k terms. Variances of independent terms add.
worked solution
  1. Each term q_i k_i has variance1 (product of two independent unit-variance values)
  2. Summing d_k independent termsvariances add → total variance = d_k
  3. Standard deviation√d_k
  4. At d_k = 512
    your turn√512 = 22.6 — measured empirically as 22.591
  5. Softmax of scores spread over ±22
    your turnone weight → 1.0000, the rest → 0.0000
answerVariances of independent terms add, so summing d_k of them gives variance d_k and standard deviation √d_k. At that spread the softmax saturates into a hard one-hot, its gradient collapses to ~0, and the layer stops learning.
What this is really testingNote it fails by not training at all, not by training badly. And early in training the position it commits to is essentially random, since the projections are still near-random.
6coremulti-head budget↗ lesson
Compare the parameter count of one attention head of size 512 against eight heads of size 64. Count only the Q, K and V projections.
worked solution
  1. One head of 5123 matrices of 512 × 512 = 3 × 262,144
  2. = 786,432
  3. One head of 643 matrices of 512 × 64 = 3 × 32,768 = 98,304
  4. Eight of those
    your turn8 × 98,304 = 786,432
answerBoth are 786,432. Splitting into heads costs nothing — the budget is redistributed, not increased.
A second way to see itEach head works in d/h dimensions, so h heads of size d/h is always the same total as one head of size d.
What this is really testingThis is why multi-head is close to a free win, and why 8–16 heads is standard. Push it much further and each head has too few dimensions left to represent anything useful.
7stretchthe cost↗ lesson
A model's context grows from 512 to 4,096 tokens. By what factor does the attention work grow? Then explain why the transformer beat the RNN despite being O(T²) against O(T).
worked solution
  1. Length factor4096 ÷ 512 = 8
  2. Work factor8² = 64
  3. RNN costO(T) operations, but strictly serial
  4. Attention cost
    your turnO(T²) operations, but all independent
  5. On parallel hardware
    your turnwall-clock time is operations ÷ how many run at once
answer64× the work. The transformer won because operation count is not wall-clock time: an RNN's T steps cost T units of time whatever hardware you own, while attention's T² operations run simultaneously.
What this is really testingA genuinely useful lesson beyond this course: asymptotic complexity assumes a sequential machine. When the hardware is massively parallel, the constant factors and the parallelisability can matter more than the exponent.
